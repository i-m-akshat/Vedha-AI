ADR-003 ACCEPTED 2026-09-24

Deterministic Work Experience Sorting with Present Employment First

💡 Plain-English Summary: Why Does This Matter?

When a hiring manager or recruiter looks at a resume, the very first question on their mind is: "What is this person doing right now?" If you recently started a brand new job in January 2026, but your resume lists your old job (Jan 2024 – Jan 2026) above your current one because an AI thought your old job sounded more relevant to the keywords, recruiters assume you made a mistake or are currently unemployed. We built an ironclad sorting rule: your current job is unconditionally pinned to the top of your resume, followed neatly by your past jobs in reverse chronological order.

1. Context & The Problem

Two distinct factors previously caused resume work experiences to display out of chronological order:

  • AI Keyword Reordering Prompt: An LLM instruction instructed the model to prioritize jobs by matching score: "- Reorder experience entries so the most JD-relevant role appears FIRST". When an older 3-year role had more keyword overlap than a 3-month current role, the LLM demoted the candidate's current employer.
  • Naive Integer Date Parsing: An older helper used int.TryParse(StartDate). On inputs like "Jan 2026" or "January 2026", integer parsing failed and returned 0, resulting in erratic sort behavior.

2. The Architectural Decision

We implemented an unyielding two-layer safety mechanism:

  • Prompt Contract Clarification: In TailorCommands.cs, we removed the recommendation to reorder based on keyword relevance. The LLM prompt now states as an absolute rule: "The candidate's CURRENT / PRESENT employment (isCurrent == true or endDate contains 'Present') MUST ALWAYS APPEAR FIRST in the experience list. All subsequent past employment entries MUST strictly follow reverse-chronological order."
  • Domain Sorting Invariant (ExperienceChronologyHelper): We created ExperienceChronologyHelper in the Domain layer (`ResumeSchema.cs`) with a three-tier comparison algorithm:
    1. Tier 1 — Current Role Priority: Evaluates isCurrent == true OR EndDate matching keywords "Present", "Current", "Now", "Ongoing". Current roles receive sort priority 1; past roles receive priority 0.
    2. Tier 2 — Current Role Tie-Breaking: If multiple current roles exist (e.g. concurrent freelance and full-time), sorted by StartDate descending.
    3. Tier 3 — Past Role Reverse-Chronology: Sorted by EndDate descending, tie-broken by StartDate descending.
  • Robust Multi-Format Date Normalization: Supports month abbreviations ("Jan 2026"), full names ("January 2026"), ISO dates ("2026-01"), slashes ("01/2026"), and 4-digit regex fallback.
  • Universal Pipeline Enforcement: NormalizeAndSortExperience() is automatically executed upon Master Resume creation/update, AI tailoring generation, PDF/Word/Markdown exports, and defensively on the frontend UI.

3. Alternatives Evaluated

❌ Relying Solely on LLM Prompting

Large Language Models are non-deterministic. An LLM may follow the prompt 98% of the time, but 2% of candidates would still receive flawed resumes. Software must be deterministic.

❌ Frontend UI-Only Sorting

Sorting only in React would fix the web preview, but generated PDF files, Word downloads, and backend ATS scores would still compute on unsorted data.

4. Consequences & Verification

  • 100% Deterministic Guarantee: Current employment always displays first regardless of LLM temperature or keyword weightings.
  • Complete Test Verification: Tested with 55 unit tests covering edge cases (concurrent jobs, string dates, missing booleans, backwards dates).
